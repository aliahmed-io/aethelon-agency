# Commerce Studio: Ask Phill Audit and Service Pricing Plan

**Prepared:** 12 August 2026  
**Positioning assumption:** Egyptian-founded, location-independent commerce studio serving ambitious international product brands remotely; currently operating from Saudi Arabia and planning a future move to Southern Europe.  
**Pricing assumption:** Global value-based pricing in USD. The founder’s nationality, accent, and lack of in-person delivery should not be used as a local-market discount. Price against the commercial value, technical difficulty, scope, risk, and quality of the delivery system.

## Executive assessment

Commerce Studio is visually and technically strong enough to present as a premium independent studio. Its Paper Signal identity is distinctive, the homepage hero now works, the service architecture is unusually coherent for a solo practice, the Insights hub is deeper than the reference in several of the requested topics, and the native App Router, SEO metadata, structured data, responsive design, dark mode, filtering, and interaction details give it a credible professional foundation.

The important distinction is between **craft quality** and **commercial proof**. Ask Phill is not only polished; it has recognizable client brands, 200+ migrations, a 60+ person team, 10+ years of experience, a case archive with many real outcomes, a qualification form, a visible service taxonomy, and a content engine tied to migration and platform-buying intent.[1] Commerce Studio must not copy those claims. It should own the opposite advantage: direct senior attention, a narrow technical point of view, fast context transfer, and a lower-overhead path to custom commerce.

My assessment is therefore: **8.3/10 for client-facing design and technical presentation, 8.5/10 for editorial/SEO foundation, and approximately 7.0/10 for commercial readiness until real client proof, founder credentials, production lead handling, and clearer engagement packages are published.** This is a strong launch position. The absence of an American accent is not a quality defect; the real commercial test is whether prospects experience clear written communication, reliable calls, strong process, and credible outcomes.

### What Commerce Studio has not missed

You have already covered the parts many independent studios omit: a strong homepage, dedicated Work/Services/About/Insights/Contact routes, a custom 404, sitemap and robots files, route metadata, canonical URLs, Open Graph and Twitter tags, Article and BreadcrumbList JSON-LD, responsive layouts, dark mode, filters, accessible gallery controls, page transitions, a newsletter flow, a contact-intake flow, and honest disclosure that the selected work is experimental rather than fabricated client work.

The Services page is especially effective. It explains custom commerce, AI commerce, 3D/AR, conversion and recovery, performance/SEO, and ongoing development while also introducing the underlying levers of strategy, UX direction, frontend systems, commerce integration, AI workflows, and growth loops. That is already comparable to a mature boutique studio’s service architecture and is more aligned with your actual offer than copying a Shopify-only agency model.[2]

### What is still missing or underpowered

The most important gaps are commercial rather than visual. The site should make the first purchase easier to understand by publishing **engagement shapes**, **starting budgets**, **typical timelines**, **what is included**, **what is excluded**, and **what happens after launch**. It should also add a founder block that gives prospects a reason to trust the person behind the work: name, current operating timezone, years of relevant experience, technical strengths, languages, and the type of product brand that is a good fit. The brand does not need a fixed physical-office claim.

The Work archive needs more real proof over time, not more invented projects. Each future case should show the initial constraint, platform context, scope, timeline, key implementation decisions, verified results, and a short client quote only when it is genuine and approved. Until then, keep the “selected experiment” label; it is an ethical advantage.

The Contact page already asks for focus, budget, timeline, and project description, but the current mock backend is not launch-ready. Connect it to a real inbox or CRM, add an expected response time, and create a simple intake confirmation. Ask Phill’s form is a useful reference because it qualifies by commercial maturity and offers email, phone, and discovery-call paths rather than one generic submission action.[5]

The Insights hub should add a small commercial-intent layer: “What does a custom storefront cost?”, “Shopify vs Next.js: which should you choose?”, “What can be built in four weeks?”, “When is AI search not worth it?”, “What does a 3D product experience require from the brand?”, and “What happens in the first 30 days after launch?” Your current evidence-backed feature articles are strong; these additional pages would convert research traffic into better-qualified conversations.

## Recommended pricing position

International agency and senior-freelance benchmarks are useful context, but they are not the right direct price target for a one-person studio without a long client track record. Ask Phill’s own 2026 headless guide describes programs starting at **EUR 100,000–150,000**, which confirms the reference agency’s scale rather than the price you should copy.[7] Your initial offer should instead be based on a bounded workload of roughly **60–110 project hours over three to four weeks**, with a solo-freelancer rate that is accessible but still protects your time.

The strategy is therefore: **sell focused outcomes, not agency-sized transformations.** Quote international clients in USD, keep the scope legible, limit the number of active projects, and use the first five paid engagements to build proof. An excellent website can justify a professional price, but without verified client outcomes it should not be priced like a mature agency with a large team.

### Founding-client rules

Use the first number as the **founding rate for the first five paid clients** and the second number as the standard rate afterward. These are user-defined launch prices rather than a uniform percentage discount. Tie every engagement to a written scope, a deposit, a mutually agreed delivery window, and permission to publish an anonymized case study or verified outcome if the client approves it.

## Service price ladder

The following prices are intentionally structured around what one person can deliver in approximately three to four weeks. They are not quotes for enterprise migrations, regulated systems, large catalogs, multi-region platforms, custom checkout infrastructure, or complex 3D asset production.

| Service | First 5 clients: founding rate | Original standard rate | Typical scope and timeline |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| AI, AR, or other scoped feature addition | **$300** | **$500** | One clearly defined feature added to an existing storefront or site. Examples include a bounded AI helper, AR/3D interaction, advanced search behavior, cart recovery improvement, or another agreed feature. Third-party/API fees are separate. |
| Standard custom ecommerce | **$1,500** | **$2,000** | 3–4 weeks: focused storefront with a defined page set, one commerce connection, core product/collection flow, responsive implementation, basic SEO, QA, and handoff. |
| Complete Growth Ultra ecommerce | **$2,500** | **$4,000** | Approximately 4 weeks: the complete agreed storefront package with the Growth Ultra feature set, launch preparation, responsive QA, basic SEO, and handoff. “All included” means all items listed in the written scope, not unlimited revisions or integrations. |
| Ongoing development retainer — 10 hours/month | **$200/mo** | **$300/mo** | Reserved capacity for small improvements, maintenance, and hot fixes, with a defined response window. Unused hours do not roll over unless agreed in writing. |
| Ongoing development retainer — 20 hours/month | **$300/mo** | **$500/mo** | A deeper one-person partnership covering small improvements, maintenance, and hot fixes, with a monthly priority list and response window. |

Strategy sprints, store audits, improvement plans, technical notes, roadmaps, handoff documentation, and other supporting documents are **included at no additional charge when they are needed to deliver one of the packages above**. They are not separate paid line items unless a client requests a standalone consulting engagement outside a build or feature package. The same inclusion applies to the planning and maintenance notes needed to perform retainer work.

### The three prices I would publish first

Do not put the entire table on the homepage. Lead with three understandable starting points and use the full ladder inside a “Work with me” or proposal page.

| Public starting point | First-five price | Standard price | Why it is useful |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **AI / AR / Feature Addition** | From **$300** | From **$500** | A low-risk entry point for one clearly scoped feature added to an existing store or site. |
| **Standard Custom Ecommerce** | From **$1,500** | From **$2,000** | A focused three-to-four-week build for a defined storefront scope. |
| **Complete Growth Ultra Ecommerce** | From **$2,500** | From **$4,000** | The strongest packaged offer, with all items listed in the written scope included. |

The public wording should be **“Founding freelancer rates — first five engagements”** rather than “discount.” Once five paid engagements are complete, remove the founding language and replace it with the standard rates or a revised “starting from” range based on actual delivery data. The founding rates are a launch structure for building proof, not compensation for your nationality, accent, or location.

## Remote delivery and accent strategy

Do not apologize for your accent or put nationality at the center of the sales narrative. International buyers purchase clarity, judgment, responsiveness, and risk reduction. Your website can make those qualities visible with a short founder video or voice note only if you are comfortable, a strong written proposal template, a transparent weekly update rhythm, shared project documentation, a calendar link with timezone conversion, and a clear response-time promise.

For calls, send a written agenda beforehand and a concise decision summary afterward. Use captions or transcripts when helpful. If a technical point is difficult to express live, say that you will document it and follow up in writing; this signals rigor rather than weakness. Lead with “remote-first, senior-led delivery” and “one direct technical owner,” not a geographic discount narrative.

The trust stack for the first five international clients should be: a polished site, a clearly scoped paid strategy sprint, a short written proposal, a milestone-based payment schedule, weekly async updates, a live staging link, a documented handoff, and permission-based case-study capture. This is how you compensate for limited brand recognition—not by underpricing the work.

## Location-flexible operating model

The public brand can stay location-neutral: **“Commerce Studio — a remote-first independent commerce practice.”** In proposals and the contact flow, disclose the practical details that affect collaboration: your current working timezone, normal response window, preferred meeting windows, invoicing currency, and the legal entity or payment method used for the engagement. When you move to Southern Europe, update those operational details without rebuilding the brand or changing the value proposition.

Do not list a permanent address unless a client, payment provider, or legal document requires it. A location-independent model can be a strength for international clients when it is paired with predictable availability, clear documentation, secure file handling, and a written escalation path. The promise is continuity of delivery, not a particular country.

## Commercial boundaries to protect margin

Every proposal should define the page/template count, integrations, product/catalog assumptions, supported regions and languages, content responsibility, QA devices, revision rounds, launch support, and post-launch warranty. The following should normally be excluded or priced separately even from the **Complete Growth Ultra** package: Shopify or hosting fees, paid APIs and model usage, third-party software, product photography, copywriting, translation, data cleanup, 3D asset modeling, paid ad management, ongoing content entry, unlimited revisions, additional storefronts, and major post-launch scope changes. “All included” should always refer to the written package scope. Retainer maintenance and hot-fix support cover the agreed site or storefront scope; new features, redesigns, migrations, and work exceeding the monthly hours are quoted separately.

For builds, use a payment schedule such as **40% to reserve the project, 30% at an agreed working milestone, and 30% before launch or handoff**. For a strategy sprint, collect full payment before the sprint begins. For retainers, invoice monthly in advance. These are commercial-structure suggestions, not legal or tax advice; use a contract and professional accounting advice appropriate to your actual tax residence, legal entity, payment provider, and client jurisdiction at the time of each engagement.

## 90-day upgrade sequence

In the first 30 days, publish the three starting points, a founder credibility block, a clear “what happens after you contact me” sequence, a timezone-aware calendar link, and a real response-time promise. In days 31–60, replace the contact mock backend with a production inbox/CRM flow, add two decision-focused Insights articles, and turn the best three experiments into more detailed “concept case studies” with explicit scope and implementation notes. In days 61–90, secure one or two paid engagements, capture baseline and post-launch measures with client permission, and convert those into the first verified case studies.

The target is not to look more like Ask Phill. The target is to match its **commercial clarity and proof discipline** while keeping Commerce Studio’s own advantage: a small, direct, technically ambitious, location-independent studio that makes custom commerce feel understandable and financially sensible.
