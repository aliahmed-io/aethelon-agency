# Knowledge hub evidence notes

## Baymard — Cart and Checkout Usability

Source: https://baymard.com/research/checkout-usability

- Baymard reports that 65% of benchmarked sites have mediocre-or-worse checkout performance, 35% are decent-or-better, and only 2% are good.
- Baymard says the average site has 32 unique checkout improvements available and that its combined usability sessions indicate a large ecommerce site could potentially improve conversion by 35% through better checkout UX.
- The page says the benchmark subset covers 344 ecommerce sites.
- The page also states that Baymard’s tracked global average cart abandonment rate currently sits at 70.19%.

Use these as directional benchmark claims, not guarantees. Phrase the 35% figure as a potential opportunity from usability improvements, not as a promised lift for every store.

## Litmus — Email Marketing ROI

Source: https://www.litmus.com/resources/email-marketing-roi

- Litmus reports that email drives an average ROI of $36 for every dollar spent, while noting that results vary by company, industry, list size, permission practices, email frequency, testing, content, and analytics.
- Litmus also reports that single opt-in programs in its analysis showed an 80% higher return than double opt-in programs, but this must be treated carefully because consent and regulatory obligations vary by jurisdiction and business model.

Use the $36 figure as an industry benchmark, not a promise. Frame newsletter performance as dependent on list quality, consent, segmentation, deliverability, and lifecycle relevance.

## Google Cloud — AI Commerce Search

Source: https://docs.cloud.google.com/retail/docs

- Google documents AI Commerce Search as a set of search and recommendation capabilities that use machine-learning models for personalized search and recommendations across websites and mobile apps.
- This is product documentation, not an independent conversion study. Use it to substantiate the capability and implementation pattern, not to promise a lift.

## Baymard — E-Commerce Search UX

Source: https://baymard.com/research/ecommerce-search

- Baymard’s study condensed search findings into 31 search UX guidelines after testing 19 leading ecommerce sites across eight verticals with users aged 21–56.
- The study reports more than 700 search-specific usability issues during testing and says more than half of its guidelines concern the search field, autocomplete, and results interactions—not only search logic.
- Baymard’s broader methodology page states 25 rounds of qualitative testing with 4,400+ participant/site sessions, 54 benchmarking rounds across 344 leading sites, 12 quantitative studies with 20,240 participants, and 275,000+ UX performance scores.

Use these as evidence that product discovery is a UX system, not merely a keyword field. Avoid implying that a semantic index guarantees conversion improvement without measurement.

## Google Merchant Center — 3D and Augmented Reality

Source: https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/13675100?hl=en

- Google says 3D models let customers see products from different angles and zoom into details.
- Google says AR can help shoppers visualize a product in a real environment before purchase and that 3D/AR products often receive more interactions than products with 2D images.
- Google notes that eligibility and availability are limited by market and product/feed conditions; the help page lists the US, Canada, Australia, Japan, and India for the described experience.

Treat this as a capability and engagement rationale, not as a guaranteed conversion or returns reduction statistic. Recommend testing 3D/AR on products where scale, fit, material, or spatial context matters most.

## Northwestern Medill Spiegel Research Center — Online Reviews

Source: https://spiegel.medill.northwestern.edu/how-online-reviews-influence-sales/

- The study reports that purchase likelihood for a product with five reviews was 270% greater than for a product with no reviews, with marginal benefit diminishing after the first five reviews.
- It reports that review display increased conversion by 190% for lower-priced products and 380% for higher-priced products in the study context.
- It reports that purchase likelihood typically peaked at ratings between 4.0 and 4.7, then declined as ratings approached 5.0.

The page says these findings were first published in 2017. Use them as directional research evidence, not current universal benchmarks. Never fabricate reviews, ratings, comments, or customer quotes; recommend verified collection, moderation, consent, and transparent display.

## Google Search Central — Ecommerce SEO and Technical Foundations

Sources:
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/include-structured-data-relevant-to-ecommerce
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/canonicalization
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/build-sitemap
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/javascript-seo-basics

- Google says structured data can improve the accuracy of its understanding of ecommerce content and highlights BreadcrumbList, Organization, Product/ProductGroup, Review, and VideoObject as relevant types.
- Google defines canonicalization as selecting the representative URL from duplicates and notes that filtered/sorted URLs can create duplicate-content variants. Canonical hints, redirects, and sitemaps should agree, but Google may choose a different canonical.
- Google recommends absolute canonical URLs in sitemaps and says a sitemap is a hint rather than a guarantee of crawling or indexing.
- Google describes JavaScript processing as crawling, rendering, and indexing; server-side or pre-rendering is still recommended because it improves speed and helps crawlers that do not run JavaScript.
- Google recommends unique descriptive titles and snippets, meaningful HTTP status codes, crawlable `<a href>` links, and avoiding soft 404s.

Use this source set for the SEO plan and implementation checklist. Do not promise rankings; define success through indexed pages, qualified impressions, clicks, Core Web Vitals, crawl health, and assisted conversions.
