# Ask Phill reference audit

Source reviewed: https://askphill.com/ (accessed 2026-08-12)

## Homepage findings

- Positioning is immediately specific: “The Shopify Plus Platinum Partner for Europe’s leading fashion, lifestyle, and beauty brands.”
- The opening experience uses a dark, high-contrast editorial hero with oversized “We build the next” typography, a layered commerce/fashion image composition, and a concise category-specific descriptor.
- The primary navigation is intentionally minimal: Menu, brand mark, and Contact. The homepage uses strong direct calls to action including “See our work,” “Get in touch,” and service discovery links.
- Trust is surfaced early through a moving/scrollable logo wall with recognizable brand marks, followed by a short positioning statement and selected case studies.
- Case-study proof is not only a card grid: it includes named brands, project labels such as Shopify migration and Shopify Plus, and a dedicated cases archive.
- Services are organized as a full-funnel capability system: Shopify Migration, Strategy, Design, Development, and Retention. Each service has a rationale paragraph and concrete sub-capabilities.
- The site explicitly communicates scale and credibility: 10+ years of experience, 200+ stores launched, and 60+ experts. These are claims from the reference site, not claims to copy into Commerce Studio without proof.
- The service narrative spans discovery through post-launch retention, including IT architecture, unified commerce, UX/UI, design systems, email design, Shopify themes, headless/Hydrogen, integrations, flows, personalisation, and email marketing.
- The homepage includes a content/insights layer with migration, platform, strategy, development, and Shopify-related editorial topics.
- The overall quality advantage is not only visual polish. It is the combination of niche positioning, proof density, service taxonomy, recognizable client evidence, platform authority, clear next steps, and a visible path from strategy to delivery to retention.

## Navigation validation

- The homepage’s extracted service link points to `/strategy`, but that URL currently returns a 404 page. The site’s actual navigation exposes `/pages/services`, `/pages/about`, `/blogs/cases`, `/blogs/blog`, `/pages/contact`, plus deeper service routes such as `/pages/ecommerce-strategy`, `/pages/branding-and-design`, and `/pages/shopify-development`.
- This is a useful quality reminder: a premium reference can still have a live routing defect. Commerce Studio’s native App Router route coverage and sitemap consistency are a relative strength, provided every published route remains smoke-tested after content changes.

## Services and About-page findings

The actual services route opens with a strong editorial statement (“Aiming high, together.”), one featured case, and a direct “Work with us” action. It repeats the service system from the homepage but adds a clearer partnership narrative, a larger client/case-study section, and a final proposal CTA. The page also exposes a visible content path for strategy, design, development, Klaviyo, and migrations.

The About page presents a real team directory with named people and roles such as Front-end Developer, Lead Developer, Product Owner, Head of Tech, and Partnership and Event Manager. This materially strengthens trust because the visitor can see delivery capacity and disciplines rather than only reading abstract capability claims. Commerce Studio is intentionally a solo studio, so it should not mimic team scale; it should make senior ownership, direct communication, and a reliable partner network explicit instead.

The contact page is a qualification tool, not just an email form. It offers email, phone, discovery-call booking, and a project form that asks for first name, last name, email, company, revenue range, and acquisition source. The revenue-range question lets Ask Phill route leads by commercial maturity. Commerce Studio currently has a simpler contact flow; adding budget/timeline/platform fields would raise lead quality without pretending to be enterprise.

The case archive is a major reference advantage. It uses a highlighted trio, a larger archive, recognizable client names, short project-specific headlines, platform/service tags, a “Load more” continuation, and case routes that cover migration, multi-store, headless, loyalty, and Shopify Plus work. Commerce Studio has six honest selected experiments and richer individual route structure, but it still has less proof density and less visible breadth than this archive.

A representative case detail page (PAL Sporting Goods) is structured around a clear before/after narrative: the previous platform was restrictive, the migration was completed in six weeks, fulfillment-partner integration risks were addressed, and the lean team gained autonomy. It gives the case a specific timeframe and operational consequence rather than only showing screenshots. Commerce Studio’s metric placeholders are honest, but the next quality step is to replace them with verified timelines, constraints, implementation decisions, and outcomes as real engagements are completed.

Ask Phill’s Knowledge hub is tightly tied to buying intent. Its visible topics include platform migrations, B2B on Shopify, headless commerce, Shopify AI tooling, Hydrogen, and cost/timeline guides. Commerce Studio’s 12-article Insights hub is broader across AI commerce, 3D/AR, cart recovery, newsletters, SEO, reviews, strategy, operations, and global commerce; it is stronger for the user’s stated offer, but should add more “decision” content that helps a prospect choose an approach, scope, budget, and timeline.

## Commerce Studio live audit

The live homepage is visually strong and clearly differentiated. The hero now renders correctly, the positioning is specific, the offer is visible above the fold, and the primary path is short: Start a project or See the work. The page also includes selected work, service levers, a solo-studio value proposition, a small interactive product proof, FAQs, and repeated contact CTAs. At a visual level it is plausibly an 8/10 premium independent-studio site, especially for editorial composition and brand consistency.

The Services page is one of Commerce Studio’s strongest competitive assets. It frames six useful levers—Strategy, UX direction, Frontend systems, Commerce integration, AI workflows, and Growth loop—then expands into Custom E-commerce, AI Commerce, 3D & AR Experiences, Conversion & Recovery, Performance & SEO, and Ongoing Development. This is comparable to Ask Phill’s strategy/design/development/retention system, while being more aligned to the actual studio offer.

The main commercial weakness is not missing technical capability. The site explains what the studio can build, but it does not yet make scope, engagement shape, starting budget, timeline, client qualification, or post-launch commercial support as concrete as a mature agency site. The next upgrade should make buying the service easier, not add more visual decoration.

The Work archive is honest and well organized: six selected experiments, clear technology labels, dual industry/service filters, individual case routes, and explicit “Proof, not promises” disclosure. This is ethically stronger than inventing client work. Compared with Ask Phill, however, it reads as a concept archive rather than a mature client-results archive. That distinction should remain visible until real client work and verified outcomes exist.

The About page successfully turns “solo” into a positioning advantage. “Small by design. Serious by default.”, direct communication, clear scope, and a three-step studio rhythm give the visitor a credible working model. Relative to Ask Phill’s named multidisciplinary team, the missing element is not a team page; it is a stronger founder credential block: who is behind the studio, relevant experience, location/time zone, technical depth, and the kind of client/problem that is a fit.

The Contact page is already stronger than a basic studio form. It states the ideal and non-ideal client, asks for name, email, company, website, focus, budget range, timeline, and project description, and offers a direct email fallback. It currently uses a mock backend, so this is a launch-readiness gap rather than a UX gap: production delivery should connect it to a real inbox/CRM and publish a clear response-time expectation.

The Insights page is a substantive differentiator. It has 12 notes, topic filters with counts, reading times, evidence-led pull statements, source-aware articles, newsletter capture, and a linked SEO note. It covers the studio’s requested offer more comprehensively than Ask Phill’s visible hub. The main missed content type is commercial decision content: “who this is for,” “what it costs,” “how long it takes,” “Shopify vs custom,” “when not to use AI/3D,” and “what the first 30 days look like.”

## Pricing benchmark notes

Inceptive Technologies’ 2026 pricing guide states broad international benchmarks of $20–$150+ per hour, $3,000–$100,000+ project pricing, and $15,000–$50,000 for ecommerce websites. It lists experience, geography, complexity, hiring model, maintenance, security, and future enhancements as major drivers. This is a general guide, not a quote for Commerce Studio, but it supports pricing a premium independent specialist above commodity local website rates.

The Egypt-specific Code95 page appeared in search results with a broad EGP 40,000–110,000 range for its web-development services, but the live page timed out during verification. It should therefore be treated only as a directional local-market signal, not a definitive source for custom headless or AI commerce pricing.

Ask Phill’s own May 2026 headless guide is unusually explicit: it says a properly executed headless Shopify build starts at EUR 100,000–150,000, a standard build is EUR 150,000–350,000, and enterprise implementations start at EUR 350,000. Its article also says headless is wrong when budget is under EUR 100,000, when launch is needed under four months, or when a simple native Shopify setup already works. These figures describe an established European agency with 200+ migrations and a delivery team, so they are not a direct target for a new solo Egyptian studio; they are useful as a ceiling and a reminder to qualify architecture honestly.

Pricing implication: Commerce Studio should sell smaller, clearly bounded systems and strategy engagements first, quote in USD and EGP, keep custom/headless/AI/3D work separate as add-on scope, and avoid positioning a solo practice as a six-figure enterprise agency before it has equivalent client proof and delivery capacity.

The Central Bank of Egypt search result showed an 11 August 2026 average market rate of approximately EGP 50.23 buy / 50.33 sell per USD, but the official page rejected direct browser access. Pricing equivalents below therefore use a rounded planning rate of **USD 1 ≈ EGP 50**, and should be updated at proposal time. The quote should be fixed in one currency, not exposed to the client as a fluctuating conversion table.

## Implications for Commerce Studio

- Commerce Studio already matches or exceeds the reference in editorial personality, asymmetric composition, custom imagery, route depth, technical SEO, and breadth of emerging commerce topics.
- The most important gap is proof architecture rather than page count: Commerce Studio needs a sharper offer hierarchy, process/engagement model, verifiable credibility markers, stronger case-study evidence, and a more explicit post-launch/retention offer.
- Commerce Studio should not imitate Ask Phill’s enterprise claims, Shopify Plus badge, client logos, or numerical scale. It should own a narrower independent-studio position around direct senior attention, custom Next.js storefronts, AI commerce, 3D/AR, and high-context builds.
